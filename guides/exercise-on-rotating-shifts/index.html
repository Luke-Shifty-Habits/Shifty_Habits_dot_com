How to exercise consistently when you work rotating shifts

Training plans assume you can go to the gym on Monday, Wednesday and Friday. On a rotating roster those days are earlies one week, nights the next and days off the week after. Here's how to make exercise fit the roster instead.

By Luke Briggs — maintenance leading hand, shift worker since 2022, and the developer of Shifty Habits

The short answer: stop planning workouts by weekday. Give each shift type its own slot — after earlies, before lates, after waking on nights, the long session on days off — and its own size of workout. Then set your target per roster cycle ("eight sessions this rotation"), not per week.

And keep a minimum version for your hardest shifts: a ten-minute walk on nights counts. It's what keeps the habit alive between the days you can train properly.

Pick a slot for each shift type

Every shift type has a natural window for movement, and they're not in the same place:

ShiftBest slotRealistic version
EarlyAfter the shift, before you slumpGym on the way home, or a 30-minute session
LateMorning, before the shiftYour best full session of the work week
NightAfter waking, before the shiftA walk or 15–20 minutes; nothing heroic
Day offWhenever suits youThe long run, the class, the big session

Write this down once and you never have to decide again. "When can I train this week?" becomes "what shift am I on?", which you already know.

Plan training around the roster, not the week

Weekly training plans fail on rotating rosters because your roster doesn't repeat every seven days. A 4-on-4-off repeats every eight; a DuPont every twenty-eight. So plan in cycles. On a 4-on-4-off with 12-hour shifts, for example:

  • Work days: one or two short sessions or walks across the four shifts, mostly as minimum versions
  • First day off: rest, or an easy walk — especially after nights
  • Middle days off: two proper sessions
  • Last day off: one lighter session, and prep for the block

That's four or five sessions every eight days, which over a month is more than most people manage with a three-times-a-week plan that keeps getting derailed. The 4-on-4-off guide lays out the whole cycle.

Keep a minimum version

The workout you skip because it's too big for the day you've had does nothing. The ten-minute walk you actually do keeps the habit's shape intact. Define a minimum version for your hardest shift — a walk round the block, ten minutes of mobility, twenty bodyweight squats — and count it as done.

On a bad night, the goal isn't fitness. It's not breaking the pattern.

Mind the sleep window

Hard exercise close to sleep makes it harder for some people to drop off, and for a shift worker sleep is the thing that can least afford to suffer. Keep intense sessions a few hours away from your main sleep — which is why the after-waking slot works so well on nights, and why a hard gym session straight after a night shift, right before bed, often doesn't.

Gentle movement is different. A short walk in daylight after an early, or round the block before bed, generally helps rather than hurts.

Remove the friction

  • A gym that's open 24 hours, or one on the commute, matters more than a nicer one across town.
  • Kit packed the night before — or the day before, on nights.
  • A home fallback: a mat and a few weights or bands for the days the gym isn't happening.
  • Movement at work where it's possible: stairs, a walk on the break.

Frequently asked questions

How can I exercise consistently when working rotating shifts?

Give each shift type its own exercise slot and its own size of workout — after earlies, before lates, after waking on nights, longer sessions on days off — and set your target per roster cycle instead of per week. Keep a minimum version, like a ten-minute walk, for your hardest shifts so the habit never fully stops.

When is the best time to work out on night shift?

For most people it's after waking and before the shift, in the afternoon or early evening. You're rested, it's well away from your next sleep, and it acts as part of your start-of-day routine. Save hard sessions for that slot and keep anything after the shift gentle.

Is it OK to exercise after a night shift?

Gentle movement such as a short walk is fine and can help you wind down. A hard workout straight after a night shift, just before you try to sleep, can make falling asleep harder for some people, so keep intense sessions to the afternoon before your next shift or to your days off.

How many workouts a week is realistic on shift work?

Think per roster cycle rather than per week. On a 4-on-4-off roster, for example, one or two short sessions across the work days and two or three proper sessions on the days off gives four or five every eight days, which is more than many people manage with a weekly plan that keeps getting derailed.
