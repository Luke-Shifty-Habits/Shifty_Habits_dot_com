Continental shift calendar (2 days, 2 nights, 4 off)

Two days, two nights, four off: what most UK sites mean by continental shifts. Set the day your cycle starts and see every shift for the year.

The short answer: The continental pattern is an eight-day cycle of two 12-hour days, two 12-hour nights and four days off. Four crews cover the clock, each averaging 42 hours a week, and the cycle drifts one weekday later every eight days.

The calendar needs JavaScript turned on. The pattern is described below.

The Continental (2-2-4) pattern

One full cycle, one square per day: D day shift, N night shift, – day off.

DDNN––––
Cycle length8 days
Shift length12 hours
Average week42 hours

It always rotates forwards, days before nights, which is the direction the body copes with better. Two nights is too few to adapt to, so most people stay on a day clock and get through the nights.

There's also an 8-hour version (two mornings, two afternoons, two nights, two off). You can build it in the calendar by changing the shift types and the cycle.

Frequently asked questions

What is a continental shift pattern?

In the UK it usually means two 12-hour day shifts, two 12-hour night shifts, then four days off, repeating every eight days and averaging 42 hours a week. The name is also used for an 8-hour version: two mornings, two afternoons, two nights, two off.

Do continental shift days off fall on the same days each week?

No. The eight-day cycle moves one weekday later every time, so your days off only land on the same weekdays again every eight weeks.

How do I see my continental roster on a calendar?

Open the calendar on this page, set the date of your first day shift as day 1, and it fills in every day shift, night shift and day off for the next twelve months. Share it as a link or print it.
